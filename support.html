Counting

How do I count from the Lock Screen?

Start counting in the app. A counter appears on your Lock Screen (and in the Dynamic Island on supported iPhones). Tap it to add a bead without unlocking. You can turn this on or off in Settings → Lock Screen → Show counter on Lock Screen inside Naamrit.

Each time the screen lights up, iPhone also wakes Face ID. That's normal. To count without the screen at all, use Earphones, Pocket Mala, your Apple Watch, or Dhyan mode, a dark full-screen counter (tap anywhere to count, press and hold to leave).

Can I count with the Action Button or from Control Center?

Yes. Naamrit includes a Count one jap action.

  • Action Button (iPhone 15 Pro and later): open iPhone Settings → Action Button. Choose Controls and pick Count one jap from Naamrit (iOS 18 or later). You can also choose Shortcut and pick Count one jap.
  • Control Center and the Lock Screen (iOS 18 or later): edit Control Center, or the two buttons at the bottom of the Lock Screen, and add Naamrit's Count one jap control.
  • Siri and Shortcuts: say "Count one jap in Naamrit", or use the Shortcuts app. "Undo jap in Naamrit" removes the last bead.

How do I count with my earphones?

On the Jap screen, tap Earphones, lock your phone and put it in your pocket. Press the earphone button once to count. A triple press undoes the last bead. This works with wired earphones, AirPods and most Bluetooth earphones or ring clickers that have a play/pause button. A soft tanpura plays during this mode (see why). You can set its volume in Settings → Earphone counting.

What is Pocket Mala and how do I use it?

Pocket Mala lets you count with your phone put away. On the Jap screen, tap Pocket. Choose one or more ways to count, then tap Start Pocket Mala:

  • Voice or whisper: chant aloud or softly, with a breath between japs.
  • Knock on your pocket: two quick taps on the phone through your pocket count as one jap. This is good for silent jap.
  • Nod with AirPods: a small nod after each jap. This needs AirPods Pro, AirPods (3rd generation) or later, or AirPods Max.

If you choose more than one, a jap picked up by two of them at once is only counted once. You can feel each bead as a light tap, hear a wooden bead click in your earphones, and have the count spoken at each mala or every 27 beads. Set a sankalp (a goal in malas) and Pocket Mala will tell you when it's complete, then stop. On rough roads, voice or nod works better than knocks.

How do I set up voice counting?

Go to Settings → Voice counting → Set up voice counting (or tap the setup option for Voice in Pocket Mala). Chant your naam five times at your normal pace, with a breath between each, so Naamrit learns your rhythm and loudness. Set it up again if you move to a different room, change mantra, or change pace. If it misses japs or counts extra, try the setup again in a quieter place.

How do I set up knock or nod counting?

In Pocket Mala, tap the setup option next to Knock on your pocket or Nod with AirPods and follow the short guide. Naamrit learns how firmly you knock or how deeply you nod. For nods, wear your AirPods and make sure they're connected first. Knock and nod use Motion & Fitness access. If Pocket Mala can't start, check that microphone and motion access are on in iPhone Settings → Naamrit.

Why does a tanpura play in Pocket Mala or Earphones mode?

iPhone only keeps an app running with the screen locked while it is playing or recording sound. It also only sends earphone button presses to an app that is playing sound. So when you count with knocks, nods or earphone buttons (without voice), Naamrit plays a soft tanpura drone to keep counting. When voice counting is on, the microphone does that job instead, so no tanpura is needed. You can change its volume in Settings → Earphone counting → Tanpura volume.

Why do I see the orange microphone dot?

iPhone shows an orange dot whenever an app is using the microphone. In Naamrit, that's while voice counting or Pocket Mala with voice is on, or while you record your own chant. Voice counting only measures loudness. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. The dot goes away when you stop Pocket Mala or voice counting. See the Privacy Policy.

Can I chant along with my own voice?

Yes. Open the chant player from the Jap screen and record yourself chanting once (up to 30 seconds). It loops over the tanpura for as long as you choose, and each repetition can be added to your mala. The recording stays on your iPhone. To remove it, tap the delete button next to it in the chant player.

Apple Watch, widgets and themes

How does the Apple Watch app sync?

Install Naamrit on your Apple Watch from the Watch app on your iPhone. On the Watch, tap the screen or turn the Digital Crown to count. On watchOS 11 and later, you can also use Double Tap (pinch your thumb and finger twice). Counts are sent straight to your iPhone. If the phone is out of range, the Watch keeps counting and syncs when they're back together. If the Watch seems out of step, open Naamrit on your iPhone. The Watch shows the same naam and mala as your phone.

How do I add widgets?

Touch and hold the Home Screen or Lock Screen, tap Edit → Add Widget (or Customize for the Lock Screen), and search for Naamrit. Home Screen widgets come in small, medium and large and show today's jap on a mala, your naam, and the day's shloka. Lock Screen widgets show your progress at a glance. Widgets use the theme you choose in the app.

How do I change the theme?

On the Jap screen, open Settings → Theme. Choose from themes such as Kesar, Vrindavan, Kailash, Ayodhya, Shakti, Ganga and Chandni, and a few night-only themes. You can also choose light, dark, or follow your iPhone.

Apple Health and your data

How does Apple Health work with Naamrit?

On the Sadhana tab, tap Connect Apple Health. Naamrit then saves each sitting (counting without a break of more than 10 minutes, at least one minute long) as Mindful Minutes. If you wore your Apple Watch, the app also shows your heart rate during the sitting, your resting heart rate and heart rate variability. These are your own measurements, not medical advice. Health data never leaves your device. To stop access, open the Health app → your profile → Apps → Naamrit.

I deleted the app or got a new phone. Can I get my counts back?

Naamrit has no account and no server. Your counts, history, settings and recorded chant live only on your device. If you delete the app, they are deleted too. They can only be restored from an iPhone backup (iCloud Backup, or a backup to your Mac or PC) made while the app was installed. When you set up a new iPhone from a backup, your Naamrit data comes with it. Mindful Minutes already saved to Apple Health stay in Health either way.

Does Naamrit need the internet?

No. All scripture, paintings, sounds and guidance are built in, and Naamrit works fully offline. The only things that use the internet are optional Dakshina purchases through the App Store, and links you choose to open, such as a helpline website.

Contact

Found a bug, a typo in a verse, or have a suggestion? Open an issue at github.com/Abhishek10879/naamrit-site/issues. Please mention your iPhone model and iOS version (and Apple Watch and watchOS version, if relevant). Issues are public, so please don't include personal or health details.

If you are in distress, please talk to someone now. In India, call 112 in an emergency. Inside the app, Granth → What's troubling you? → Talk to someone lists helplines you can call.
